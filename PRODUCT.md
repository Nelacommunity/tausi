# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Tanzanian secondary and university students studying across subjects (Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Computer Science, Economics, Accounting, History, Geography, Law, and more). They use Tausi on mobile and desktop, often under time pressure — preparing for exams, completing assignments, checking their own work. Many are bilingual in English and Swahili. Free-tier users are budget-conscious; premium users are serious about results.

Educators and tutors are a secondary audience: uploading past papers, generating marking schemes, reviewing student output.

## Product Purpose

Tausi is an AI study platform built for East African students. It generates practice exams from syllabi or past papers, marks student answers with detailed feedback, supports AI-assisted writing and essay development, and provides a library of past papers organized by subject. Success looks like a student walking into an exam more confident because Tausi helped them practice the right things.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, clear, empowering. The voice is direct and knowledgeable — like a sharp tutor who respects the student's intelligence. No hand-holding, no infantilizing encouragement. It gives you what you need and gets out of the way. Bilingual by design: English and Swahili are equals, not an afterthought.

## Anti-references

- **Generic Western SaaS**: navy-blue sans-serif "empower your team" startup aesthetic. Tausi is not a B2B productivity tool.
- **Generic dark-mode AI tools**: purple-on-dark, glowing orb blurs, "powered by AI" badge-everything aesthetic. The current landing page is too close to this — indigo orb blurs on gray-950 is exactly the reflex to break.
- **Bloated university portals**: institutional gray, cluttered navigation, form-heavy bureaucratic UI.
- **Gamified edu-apps**: Duolingo-style streaks, cartoon mascots, excessive celebration states. Students use this seriously.

## Design Principles

1. **Respect the student's intelligence.** No patronizing copy, no fake enthusiasm. Information density should feel earned, not cluttered.
2. **Local first.** Design choices should feel right for East Africa — not adapted from a Western SaaS template. Color, type, and tone should feel native to the context, not imported.
3. **Clarity over decoration.** The product's value is in the output (an exam, a marked paper, a study set). UI that competes with that output is a failure.
4. **Both surfaces, one identity.** The landing page and the app should feel like the same product — not a marketing site stapled to a different tool.
5. **Bilingual without compromise.** Language switching must feel seamless. Layout, spacing, and type must handle both English and Swahili copy with equal grace.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Mobile-first — many Tanzanian students access on Android phones with smaller screens. Reduced-motion support required. Bilingual UI (English/Swahili) must not break layout. Consider students in low-bandwidth environments: no heavy animations or large assets that degrade performance.
